Serta was supposed to settle it. Instead the market treated the decision as a
drafting problem, and the drafting problem got solved — for the sponsors.

## What actually changed

The open-market-purchase exception is now the most negotiated three lines in a
term loan. What used to be boilerplate is where the whole fight happens.

- **Sacred rights** got wider, but not wide enough to cover priority.
- **Pro rata sharing** provisions moved from the "everyone consents" bucket to
  the "required lenders" bucket in a surprising number of 2023–2025 vintage deals.
- **Cooperation agreements** became the practical defense. If a majority can't
  be assembled, the exercise doesn't happen.

## The part people underrate

An uptier is a *coalition* problem before it is a *documents* problem. The
documents tell you what a majority can do. They tell you almost nothing about
whether a majority exists.

> The cheapest protection in the market is a signed co-op agreement executed
> before anyone knows there's a deal.

## Where this goes

Expect the next round of litigation to be about the co-op agreements themselves —
whether they're enforceable, whether joining one is a breach of anything, and
what happens to the lender who defects at the last minute.
