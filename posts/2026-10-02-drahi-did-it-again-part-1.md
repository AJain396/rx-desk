Altice USA was formed by Patrick Drahi’s Altice through two core cable deals: a purchase of Cequel (Suddenlink) in December, 2015 and then Cablevision (behind Optimum) in June, 2016. These required a large debt load (like most of Drahi’s activity), 8.6 billion for Cablevision alone.

Back in May, 2024, Bloomberg released that Altice USA (now Optimum Communications) was working with Moelis on options to approach its roughly $25 billion in debt. There weren’t any large maturities coming up, but the market didn’t response well: shares fell as much as 10.5% that day.

In this case, lenders organized early. In July 2024, a group signed an 18-month cooperation agreement. They formed a “co-op” where the lenders agreed not to cut side deals so Altice couldn’t approach each individually. Drahi’s history with lenders (See Part 3) made this a wise move.

From there, the talks essentially went like this:

- January 2025: Bloomberg reported confidential talks on a discounted debt exchange, where some creditors would take a loss. Moelis advised the company, and PJT Partners and Akin Gump advised the lenders.
- April 11, 2025: CSC Holdings (the main borrower) disclosed that it hadn't reached a deal with the Akin/PJT group, and that no talks were ongoing.
- September 9, 2025: Altice USA dropped Moelis and Ropes & Gray, and hired Evercore and Kirkland & Ellis to revive the talks. By then, the debt pile was about $26 billion.

At face, that switch in bankers was small. However, it was more of a signal of change in strategy. Moelis has been looking for a discounted exchange in January 2025, but with nothing big due till 2028, there was little interest in a deal. The co-op stood strong .

We're seeing a pattern there. When a co-op holds together, the company can either give the lenders more or change its approach, and Altice chose to change its approach (Part 2).

## Deal Parties

- Borrower: Altice USA, Inc. (now Optimum Communications, Inc.) / CSC Holdings, LLC. No bankruptcy filing.
- Financial Advisors / Bankers: Company: Moelis (reported May 2024, dropped September 2025), replaced by Evercore. Lenders: PJT Partners.
- Major Creditors / Creditor Groups: co-op / ad hoc group of term loan and note holders. Members were not named in the sources for this period (Apollo, Ares, BlackRock and Oaktree were later named in Optimum's 2025 lawsuit, see Part 2).
- Law Firms: Company: Ropes & Gray (dropped September 2025), replaced by Kirkland & Ellis. Lenders: Akin Gump.
