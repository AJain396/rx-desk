In 2000, Reed Hastings and Marc Randolph offered to sell Netflix to Blockbuster for $50 million. Blockbuster said no.

We’d say otherwise in retrospect, but back then, it was more than reasonable. Blockbuster, a giant, had about 9,000 stores at peak. Yet, by the end of 2010, Netflix stood with +12 million subscribers as Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy.

The easy version of the story is a chain: Blockbuster → Netflix → the Internet → AMC, each one “weakened”, if not “killed” by the next. The real story is a bit messier:

- Netflix: not dying, but not on top anymore either. In January 2026, YouTube led Nielsen's distributor rankings with 12.5% of all TV viewing, while Netflix had 8.8%, good for #3.
- AMC: weakened, not killed off. Struggling, it swapped debt for equity and sold new stock, which roughly doubled share count in around a year. In July, S&P upgraded it from CCC+ to B-.

So… we’re seeing that disruption isn’t an immediate finisher for a company. Rather, revenue is eaten, year after year, till that point when debt comes due and there isn’t enough of the business left to pay creditors.

On that note, we might think of who the next “Blockbusters” are:

- Chegg: the popular study tool for students. Its stock hit $113.51 in February 2021, and by late 2025 it was down 99%. Revenue dropped from $617.6 million in 2024 to $376.9 million in 2025. Chegg blames these struggles on Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and other AI models. Following two layoff rounds in 2025 (the second round cut about 45% of staff), we’ve seen Chegg shift operationally to selling skill courses to businesses.
- Getty Images: Back in early 2025, Getty told debt investors that AI wasn’t a threat. On September 1st of this year, it chose to use a 30-day grace period rather than paying interest on unsecured notes. It had $2.07 billion of debt against $51.6 million of cash at the end of June, and S&P cut it to CCC.

The thing about all of this is that these companies weren’t blindsided. Blockbuster did launch an online service and Chegg has built its own AI tools. The issue is that neither reacted fast enough before debt got too heavy.
