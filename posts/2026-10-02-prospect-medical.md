Hospitals are supposed to be a "safe" credit. Relatively recession-proof, at least on paper. Prospect Medical Holdings, which Leonard Green owned for 11 years, still ended up liquidating.

On January 11, 2025, Prospect filed Chapter 11 in the Northern District of Texas with 16 hospitals in four states and roughly 2.3 billion of funded debt. From early on, the plan was to sell the hospitals and wind down.

How it got there:

- Leonard Green (2010–2021): the majority owner. It purchased a majority stake in Prospect, followed by expansion from California to Texas and the East Coast of the U.S. This expansion focused on hospitals whose patients were mostly Medicare/Medicaid/uninsured individuals. Margins were thin.
- In 2018, Prospect borrowed to pay a 457 million dividend. Owners took cash as the business stagnated: About 257 million going to Leonard Green shareholders and roughly 90 million to CEO Sam Lee.
- Medical Properties Trust (2019): in August 2019, Prospect sold its hospital real estate to Medical Properties Trust (MPT) and leased it back.
- Recieved cash upfront, but now owed rent forever.
- 2023: MPT forgave about 646 million of what Prospect owed, in exchange for 49% of the physician business.
- Covid: contract nurse rates jumped, cyber-threats to medical centers became apparent, and pensions stood underfunded.

By the petition date, MPT held first-priority liens on the real estate, rents, equity and personal property. So at face value, little was left for unsecured creditors. Prospect filed on January 11, 2025 in the Northern District of Texas: 16 hospitals and about 2.3 billion of funded debt.

The unsecured creditors' committee was appointed on January 29, 2025, with Paul Hastings as counsel. What it did:

- March 17, 2025: objected to the proposed sale process, arguing it gave MPT "overly generous" terms.
- March 20, 2025: the court approved a global settlement with MPT. It had negotiated the deal and ended up supporting it.

The trade: the plan created a General Unsecured Claims (GUC) Trust to hold the estate's claims, and the court approved 10 million to pursue them. Leonard Green, Sam Lee and former executive David Topper are the main targets.

The plan was confirmed December 15, 2025, and went effective March 6, 2026.

In this case, it looks like the committee made the right choice. MPT had liens on real estate, rents, and equity while admin/priority claims stood ahead of unsecured (would have just burned the 10 million in estate cash fighting this)

Rather, the 2018 dividend seems to be a ripe target, and that's where the trust is pointed.

Those claims won't be easy since the Bankruptcy Code's fraudulent-transfer look-back (§548) is only 2-years. The 2018 dividend would have to be pursued based on state-laws.

For Prospect's unsecured creditors, a lot now comes down to how a court looks at a dividend from 2018.

## Deal Parties

- Debtor(s): Prospect Medical Holdings, Inc. and its hospital affiliates ("HospitalCo"), Case No. 25-80002 (SGJ), Bankr. N.D. Tex. The physician business (PHP Holdings, "PhysicianCo") was not a debtor.
- Financial Advisors / Bankers: Company: Houlihan Lokey (investment banker), Alvarez & Marsal (financial advisor). Committee: not disclosed in the sources reviewed.
- Major Creditors / Creditor Groups: Medical Properties Trust (landlord, secured lender and junior DIP lender); JMB Capital Partners ($100 million DIP); eCapital Healthcare ($90 million ABL DIP); PBGC; PhysicianCo term lenders (Centerbridge and Blue Torch provided the 2023 financing); Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors.
- Law Firms: Company: Sidley Austin (restructuring counsel), Katten Muchin Rosenman (conflicts counsel). Committee: Paul Hastings.
