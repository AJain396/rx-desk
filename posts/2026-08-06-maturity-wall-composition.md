Every maturity wall gets described the same way: a wall. The framing does the analysis no favors. A wall implies the problem is height. The problem is almost never height.

## The size, first

More than $700 billion of US high yield matures across 2027–2029, with over $350 billion of that landing in 2029 alone. Add leveraged loans and speculative-grade maturities, and 2028 alone runs to roughly $1 trillion.

Big numbers. But 2025 high yield issuance was north of $325 billion, up 17% and the strongest year since 2021. On volume alone, the market clears this. Three years of ordinary issuance covers three years of maturities. If refinancing were purely a capacity question, there'd be nothing to discuss.

## The composition is the whole problem

Two facts from 2025 issuance sit uncomfortably together. Over 70% of it was refinancing, the highest share since 2009. And straight CCC paper was under 2% of issuance, the second-lowest share on record.

Read those in sequence. The market spent 2025 doing almost nothing but refinancing, and it refinanced almost nothing rated CCC.

Everything that could term out did term out. What's left in front of 2027 and 2028 is the residual: the credits that couldn't clear a market that was wide open and doing record refi volume.

## Why that matters more than the total

A maturity wall made of BB paper is a scheduling exercise. A maturity wall made of what's left after three years of adverse selection is a default pipeline.

The distribution has been sorted, deliberately, by an open market. The issuers approaching 2027 unrefinanced are disproportionately the ones who tried and couldn't, or who looked at the clearing spread and decided to wait. Neither group's situation improves by waiting.

## What actually breaks first

Not the 2027 maturities. The springing covenants and cash sweeps that trigger 12 to 18 months ahead of them, and the auditor conversations about going concern that start around the same time.

Which means the activity shows up in late 2026 and through 2027, not on the maturity dates themselves. Distressed situations get created by the approach to a wall, not the collision with it. By the time a bond actually matures, the outcome was usually decided a year earlier, in a room with a lender group.

## The practical read

Stop tracking wall totals. Track the ratings distribution of what's still outstanding by maturity year, the refi attempts that get pulled — deals that launched and didn't price — and extension activity, which is the tell that a full refi wasn't available.

The aggregate number tells you the market's capacity. Only the composition tells you who's going to need something other than a refinancing.
