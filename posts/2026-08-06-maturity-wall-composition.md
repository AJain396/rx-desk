A wall. That's the word everyone reaches for, and the framing does the analysis no favors, a wall implies the problem is height, and the problem is almost never height.

## The size, first

More than $700 billion of US high yield matures across 2027–2029, with over $350 billion of that landing in 2029 alone. Add leveraged loans and speculative-grade maturities, and 2028 alone runs to roughly $1 trillion. Big numbers, undeniably. But 2025 high yield issuance was north of $325 billion, up 17% and the strongest year since 2021, and on volume alone the market clears this: three years of ordinary issuance covers three years of maturities. If refinancing were purely a capacity question, there would be nothing left to discuss.

It isn't.

## The composition is the whole problem

Two facts from 2025 issuance sit uncomfortably together. Over 70% of it was refinancing, the highest share since 2009. And straight CCC paper was under 2% of issuance, the second-lowest share on record. Read those in sequence, not in isolation: the market spent 2025 doing almost nothing but refinancing, and it refinanced almost nothing rated CCC. Everything that could term out did term out. What's left in front of 2027 and 2028 is the residual, the credits that couldn't clear a market that was wide open and running record refi volume.

## Why that matters more than the total

A maturity wall made of BB paper is a scheduling exercise. A maturity wall made of what survives three years of adverse selection is a default pipeline. The distribution has been sorted, deliberately, by an open market, and the issuers approaching 2027 unrefinanced are disproportionately the ones who tried and couldn't, or who looked at the clearing spread and decided to wait. Neither group's situation improves by waiting longer.

## What actually breaks first

Not the 2027 maturities themselves. The springing covenants and cash sweeps that trigger 12 to 18 months ahead of them. The auditor conversations about going concern that start around the same time. Which means the real activity shows up in late 2026 and through 2027, not on the maturity dates on the calendar. Distressed situations get created by the approach to a wall, not the collision with it; by the time a bond actually matures, the outcome was usually decided a year earlier, in a room with a lender group.

## The practical read

Stop tracking wall totals. Track the ratings distribution of what's still outstanding by maturity year. Track the refi attempts that get pulled, deals that launched and didn't price. Track extension activity, the tell that a full refi wasn't available.

The aggregate number tells you the market's capacity. Only the composition tells you who actually needs something other than a refinancing. Not a wall. A sorting problem, wearing a wall's name.
