Every maturity wall gets described the same way — a wall — and the framing does
the analysis no favors. A wall implies the problem is height. The problem is
almost never height.

## The size, first

More than **$700 billion** of US high yield matures across **2027–2029**, with
over **$350 billion** of that landing in 2029 alone. Add leveraged loans and
speculative-grade maturities in 2028 alone run to roughly **$1 trillion**.

Big numbers. But 2025 high yield issuance was **north of $325 billion**, up 17%
and the strongest year since 2021. On volume alone, the market clears this.
Three years of ordinary issuance covers three years of maturities. If refinancing
were purely a capacity question, there would be nothing to discuss.

## The composition is the whole problem

Two facts from 2025 issuance sit uncomfortably together:

- **Over 70% of it was refinancing** — the highest share since 2009.
- **Straight CCC paper was under 2% of issuance**, the second-lowest share on
  record.

Read those in sequence. The market spent 2025 doing almost nothing but
refinancing, and it refinanced almost nothing rated CCC.

Everything that could term out did term out. What remains in front of 2027 and
2028 is the residual — the credits that couldn't clear a market that was wide
open and doing record refi volume.

## Why that matters more than the total

A maturity wall made of BB paper is a scheduling exercise. A maturity wall made
of what's left after three years of adverse selection is a default pipeline.

The distribution has been sorted, deliberately, by an open market. The issuers
approaching 2027 unrefinanced are disproportionately the ones who tried and
couldn't, or who looked at the clearing spread and decided to wait. Neither
group's situation improves by waiting.

## What actually breaks first

Not the 2027 maturities. The **springing covenants and cash sweeps** that trigger
12 to 18 months ahead of them, and the auditor conversations about going concern
that start roughly the same time.

Which means the activity shows up in **late 2026 and through 2027**, not on the
maturity dates themselves. Distressed situations are created by the approach to a
wall, not the collision with it. By the time a bond actually matures, the
outcome was decided a year earlier in a room with a lender group.

## The practical read

Stop tracking wall totals. Track:

1. **Ratings distribution** of what's still outstanding by maturity year.
2. **Refi attempts pulled** — the deals that launched and didn't price.
3. **Extension activity**, which is the tell that a full refi wasn't available.

The aggregate number tells you the market's capacity. Only the composition tells
you who's going to need something other than a refinancing.
