Evercore and Kirkland came in during September 2025, with a goal of getting talks with the lenders rolling again. Yet, what we saw over the next year was mostly Optimum (Altice USA renamed to Optimum Communications in November, 2025) finding ways to raise money without needing the co-op’s signature:

- November 25, 2025: $2 billion of UnSub term loans at a fixed 9%, due November 2028, were borrowed by subsidiaries outside the main CSC credit group. The same week, Optimum sued Apollo, Ares, BlackRock and other lenders over their co-op
- January 12, 2026: another $1.1 billion of UnSub loans borrowed, also at 9%.
- March 2026: Wall Street trade groups backed the lenders' push to get the antitrust suit dismissed.
- June 1, 2026 (announced): Optimum East and a 50.01% stake in Lightpath moved into a new unrestricted subsidiary, CSC Investments II, which sold $300 million of preferred units (13% cash, or 15% if compounded). Evercore was placement agent.
- Same deal: Drahi's entities traded their common stock for $200 million of these preferred units, priced at $2.50 a share. Public shareholders were also offered the same $2.50 in a tender.
- July 6, 2026: the UnSub loans got topped up again, this time by $250 million (still 9%).

It seems like the June deal is what seems to have lenders the angriest. Optimum's June figures put Optimum East at roughly $2.0 billion of adjusted EBITDA for 2025. What's still inside the restricted group made roughly $1.1 billion, with $21.8 billion of funded debt sitting on it.

Optimum noted that if a restructuring ends up deconsolidating CSC, the tax bill could very easily be over $4 billion. Essentially, if lenders push for a deal and set off the tax bill, they'd be cutting at their own recovery too.

- Going concern: the Q2 10-Q flagged "substantial doubt," pointing to $4.1 billion due in April 2027 and $2.2 billion in July 2027, against about $1.3 billion of cash.
- NYSE: in August, the stock's 30-day average closing price fell below $1.
- Shaky Sources: in September, Optimum said earlier financials cannot be relied on because of income tax accounting errors, with no impact on cash or EBITDA.
- Lawsuits: in June, a shareholder sued Drahi in Delaware over the transactions, and on September 28, Bloomberg reported that creditors have sued Optimum and Drahi, alleging "fraudulent" deal-making.

Evercore's first year was mostly about financing around the lenders. It bought time, but CSC still has $4.1 billion due in April 2027, and the 10-Q says there's no committed financing lined up to pay it.

As of now, it seems like Evercore did "revive" things, just not the talks.

## Deal Parties

- Borrower: Optimum Communications, Inc.  (Altice USA) / CSC Holdings, LLC
  UnSub borrowers Cablevision Litchfield, LLC and CSC Optimum Holdings, LLC unrestricted subsidiary CSC Investments II LLC. No bankruptcy filing.
- Financial Advisors / Bankers: Company: Evercore (hired September 2025; placement agent with Altman Solon on the June 2026 preferred). Lenders: PJT Partners (last confirmed April 2025).
- Major Creditors / Creditor Groups: co-op including Apollo, Ares, BlackRock and Oaktree (named in Optimum's lawsuit). JPMorgan Chase Bank is the agent on the UnSub loans.
- Law Firms: Company: Kirkland & Ellis (hired September 2025, not reconfirmed since); White & Case and Quinn Emanuel on the June 2026 transactions. Lenders: Akin Gump (last confirmed April 2025). Counsel on the antitrust suit and the September 2026 creditor suit: not reconfirmed.
