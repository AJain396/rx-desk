A DIP is, on paper, the safest loan in finance: superpriority administrative
claim, priming liens, court-supervised budget, and a borrower who cannot spend a
dollar without your consent. Recoveries are near-universal.

And yet.

## The pricing is buying something else

What the coupon is actually buying:

| Component | What it's really for |
|---|---|
| Headline spread | Optionality on the plan |
| Upfront / exit fees | Compensation for the roll-up fight |
| Milestones | Control of the timeline |
| Roll-up ratio | Repricing the prepetition position |

The economics that matter are rarely in the interest rate. A 2:1 roll-up moves
more value than 300bps ever will.

## Why the auction rarely works

Third-party DIP proposals are structurally disadvantaged. The prepetition
lenders can consent to priming; a stranger has to litigate for it. That asymmetry
is worth more than any spread differential, and everyone in the room knows it.
